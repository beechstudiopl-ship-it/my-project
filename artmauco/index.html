Philippe
Tougard-Maucotel

Philippe Tougard-Maucotel (1940–2020) was a French sculptor and poet of language whose work transformed words into physical forms.

Through humor, metaphor, and imagination, he created a unique artistic universe where language became sculpture and sculpture became a way of thinking.

His works invite us to look beyond the visible — to discover unexpected connections between words, objects, and ideas.

The Artist’s World

On the North Fork of Long Island, Philippe created a place where art, nature, and everyday life became inseparable.

His home and studio in Cutchogue preserve not only his sculptures, but also the environment in which they were imagined and created. Throughout the surrounding landscape, his works continue to exist in dialogue with light, seasons, and nature.

Art Mauco

Today, Art Mauco safeguards Philippe Tougard-Maucotel’s artistic legacy. The collection includes more than 200 sculptures, drawings, archives, and works that reveal the evolution of his lifelong exploration of language as sculptural form.

Visitors are invited to discover this unique artistic universe through private visits by appointment.

About the Artist

Philippe
Tougard-Maucotel

1940 – 2020 · Paris — New York — Cutchogue

Philippe Tougard-Maucotel (1940–2020) was a French artist who made language the primary material of his sculptural practice. He was known as a “sculptor of words.”

He was born on April 27, 1940, near Paris. He studied literature at the Sorbonne and, in his early years, became associated with the Nouveau Réalisme movement, developing friendships with artists such as Raymond Hains, Jacques Villeglé, and Gérard Deschamps.

From 1963, he lived in New York, where he worked as a French teacher while continuing his artistic practice in Cutchogue, Long Island. He created word-based sculptures in wood, metal, concrete, and plywood, exploring the ambiguity of language and the play of meaning.

His work was exhibited in both the United States and France, including the exhibition “WORD” in Houston (2006), alongside artists such as Ed Ruscha and Yoko Ono, and later in 2013 in France with Gérard Deschamps.

He left behind a body of work of more than two hundred sculptures, forming a consistent exploration of language as a sculptural material.

Critical Perspective

Ita Haręza

2026

“Tougard-Maucotel’s sculptures occupy the space between language and matter — each work is simultaneously a word spoken and a silence held.”

The artist’s relationship to language began not in the studio but in the classroom, where he taught French literature for three decades. There is a generosity in this work — an invitation to complete, to interpret, to return.

Bianka Rolando

2024

“In Philippe Maucotel’s work, there is a fascination with words and their possible forms, which do not close meaning into fixed form but rather function as attempts at materialization.”

Philippe Maucotel was born in 1940 and died in 2020. He studied literary theory at the Sorbonne in Paris. He emigrated to the United States in 1962 and worked until the end of his life in his studio in Cutchogue.

His fascination with sculpture began with literature — with poetry that may focus on the observation of a single word or even a single letter. In his sculptural practice, language is not a vehicle for meaning but its very substance.

House & Studio

In 1986, Philippe purchased an old, dilapidated barn in Cutchogue, on the North Fork of Long Island, New York, surrounded by farmland and sod farms. Initially conceived as an artistic retreat and studio, the property gradually became his permanent home following his retirement from teaching.

The house and its surroundings became an integral part of Philippe’s artistic vision. Throughout the two-acre property, he placed sculptures in dialogue with the natural landscape, allowing the changing seasons, light, and weather to become part of the experience. Many of these works remain in situ today.

Philippe transformed the barn into a studio adapted to his artistic practice. It was here that he created his sculptures and developed his distinctive exploration of language as sculptural form, working in the space until his death in 2020.

Today, the studio houses the majority of Philippe Maucotel’s works and preserves the environment in which they were conceived and created.

Bożena Skuriat-Królik, Philippe Tougard-Maucotel’s longtime partner, is devoted to preserving his artistic legacy. She is the founder of Art Mauco, where she serves as an art conservator, exhibition organizer, and guide at the Art Mauco Gallery and Studio.

Bożena pours her heart into promoting Philippe’s work, symbolically defying the message of one of his sculptures, Oublié (“Forgotten”), by ensuring that he is never forgotten.

To visit the place where Philippe Tougard-Maucotel lived and worked, and to experience the deeper meanings embodied in his word sculptures, please to arrange a visit at the Art Mauco Gallery and Studio.
